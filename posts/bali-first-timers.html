Bali looks nothing like the Instagram videos. Here's the itinerary that actually works.

By Swami Chavan · Published 4 October 2026 · 9 min read

Every first-timer lands in Denpasar with the same plan: Ubud, rice terraces, swing over the jungle, sunset at Uluwatu. And every first-timer comes home with the same complaint — "it was crowded, overpriced, and nothing looked like the photos." I've done Bali four times, the last one on ₹65,000 all-in including flights. The island isn't ruined. The standard plan is. Here's the one that works.

Day 1–2: Canggu, not Seminyak

Seminyak is where Bali goes to charge you Delhi prices for average food. Canggu, 20 minutes north, has the same beach, better cafes, and warungs where nasi campur still costs 25,000 IDR (₹135). Rent a scooter for 70,000 IDR/day (₹380). Do your surfing lesson here — 350,000 IDR (₹1,900) for two hours, half the Seminyak rate.

Day 3–4: Ubud — go early, go deep

Tegalalang Rice Terrace at 7 AM is a postcard. At 11 AM it's a queue. The famous jungle swings near Tegalalang cost 150,000–350,000 IDR; walk 10 minutes further to smaller farms and it drops to 100,000 IDR with nobody in your photo. The Campuhan Ridge Walk is free and the best hour you'll spend in Ubud — go at sunrise before the heat.

Contrarian call: skip the Sacred Monkey Forest. Getting your sunglasses stolen by a monkey is not a cultural experience. Spend those two hours at the Tirta Empul water temple instead — 50,000 IDR entry, and actually worth it.

Day 5: Nusa Penida, done right

Kelingking Beach is the photo everyone comes for. Do it as a day trip from Sanur (fast boat 300,000–350,000 IDR return). Leave on the 6:30 AM boat. By 9 AM you're at the viewpoint with ten people; by 11 there are two hundred. Do Kelingking first, then Angel's Billabong, then Broken Beach. That order beats the tour buses.

Day 6–7: Uluwatu coast

Finish in Uluwatu — cliffs, proper surf beaches (Padang Padang, Bingin), and the Kecak fire dance at Uluwatu temple at sunset (150,000 IDR, book the front section). The last day is Melasti or Green Bowl beach with zero plan. That's the Bali you came for.

What things actually cost (October 2026)

Bali for 7 days from India, mid-budget: flights ₹22,000–30,000 return, stay ₹2,000–3,500/night for a good double room, food ₹800–1,200/day if you mix warungs and cafes, scooter plus petrol ₹3,000 total, activities ₹8,000–12,000. Realistic total: ₹60,000–75,000 per person including flights. Anyone quoting you ₹40,000 all-in is selling you a 4 AM layover in Kuala Lumpur.

The three mistakes every Indian first-timer makes

1. Booking Kuta as the base. Kuta is Bali's Paharganj — loud, dense, and charmless by day. You lose an hour to traffic everywhere. 2. No international roaming or eSIM sorted. A 10-day eSIM costs ₹800 on Airalo; buying a local Telkomsel SIM at the airport costs 300,000 IDR and an hour of your life. Sort the eSIM before you fly. 3. Only USD cash. Your Indian debit card works at ATMs everywhere (inform your bank first). Carry a small USD backup for the visa-on-arrival counter: USD 500,000 IDR ≈ 50 USD equivalent — payable in IDR, USD, or card.

Visa for Indians, in one paragraph

Indians get visa on arrival: 500,000 IDR (~₹2,700), 30 days, extendable once. You'll need: passport valid 6+ months, return ticket, proof of funds occasionally asked. The counter takes 20 minutes at DPS. Pay in IDR or card to avoid the money-changer spread. Official details: imigrasi.go.id.

FAQ

Is Bali safe for solo Indian travellers? Yes — violent crime is rare. The real risk is traffic. Wear the helmet, don't drink and ride, and your biggest danger is a scooter scrape on a wet road.

How many days are enough? Seven is the sweet spot. Five rushes Ubud; ten and you'll want to add Nusa Penida properly or Lombok.

Best month to go? May–September (dry season). October is the shoulder — cheaper, occasional showers, mostly fine.

Do people speak Hindi/English? English works everywhere in tourist areas. Hindi surprisingly works too in Seminyak and Kuta — a lot of Indian tourists keep the ecosystem running.
