UPI Scams in India: The Seven Patterns That Actually Work on People

By Swami Chavan·Published 2026-11-04·8 min read

Nobody loses money on UPI because the technology failed. They lose it because someone on the phone was convincing, urgent and specific. Understanding the patterns matters more than understanding the app, because every one of these scams exploits a normal human reflex.

Pattern one: the collect request

You are selling something online. The buyer says they are sending money and asks you to approve a 'request'. Approving a collect request sends money, it does not receive it. This single misunderstanding accounts for a large share of losses.

Pattern two: the 'wrong transfer' refund

Money appears in your account, then a caller claims it was sent by mistake and asks you to return it. The original transfer is often reversed later because it came from a compromised account. Return it through the bank's dispute process, never by sending a fresh transfer.

Pattern three: the QR code that pays

A QR code cannot receive money into your account. Scanning one and entering your PIN always sends money out. Anyone sending you a QR code to 'receive a payment' is trying to make you pay.

Pattern four: the fake support call

A caller claims to be from your bank, a wallet app or a delivery company and asks you to install a screen-sharing app to 'verify' something. Once they can see your screen, they can read OTPs. No legitimate support agent needs screen access or your PIN.

Pattern five: the urgency script

Your electricity will be cut in ten minutes. Your KYC expires tonight. Your parcel is held. Urgency exists to prevent you from checking. Any message that demands action within minutes is the scam, regardless of how official it looks.

Pattern six: the investment group

A WhatsApp group shows daily profits and a 'mentor' asks for a small deposit to unlock withdrawals. The app showing your balance is a mock-up. Nobody can withdraw, and the deposit is the product.

Pattern seven: the SIM swap and the OTP read aloud

If someone asks you to read out an OTP, the conversation is over. OTPs exist precisely because the person asking does not have your phone. Legitimate staff never ask for them, and no refund, delivery or verification requires one.

The one rule that defeats all seven

Money moves out of your account only when you approve a collect request, scan a QR code and enter your PIN, or share an OTP. Nothing else. If a conversation is heading towards any of those three actions, stop. Not pause — stop. Then call the number on the back of your card, not the one in the message.

The verdict

UPI is safe when you remember one sentence: money leaves only when you approve, scan or share. Every scam in this list needs one of those three from you. Refuse, and the scam has nowhere to go.

Quick answers

Can I get my money back after a UPI scam?

Sometimes. Report immediately to your bank and on the national cybercrime portal, and file an FIR for larger amounts. Recovery is more likely when reported within hours, before the funds are moved onward.

Does approving a collect request send or receive money?

It sends money. Approving a collect request authorises a debit from your account. This is the most common misunderstanding behind UPI losses.

Are screen-sharing apps dangerous for banking?

Yes. Any app that lets another person see your screen also lets them see OTPs and account details. No bank or wallet company needs screen sharing to help you.
