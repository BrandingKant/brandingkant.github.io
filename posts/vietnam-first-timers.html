Vietnam for first-timers: 7 days, ₹40,000, and the pho is not the point

By Swami Chavan · Published 4 October 2026 · 9 min read

Vietnam is the answer to every Indian traveller who's done Thailand and wants the next thing — better food, half the crowd, one-third the prices, and the most beautiful train rides in Asia. I've done Hanoi to Hoi An twice now. Seven days is enough to understand why people move here. Here's the working plan.

Getting in: e-visa, 90 days, done in your pyjamas

Indians apply at the official portal evisa.gov.vn — 25 USD single entry, 3 working days, 90 days allowed. Any agency charging 3,000 rupees is selling you a 5-minute web form. Upload a passport photo and a passport scan, pay, wait. That's it.

The 7-day route that works

Day 1–3: Hanoi. The Old Quarter is organised chaos — 36 streets named after the guilds that ran them in the 15th century. Bun cha at 50,000 VND (₹170), egg coffee at 40,000 VND (₹135), and the night train thinking time by Hoan Kiem Lake. Day 3 evening: the sleeper train to Da Nang (or fly — 45 min, ₹3,000). Day 4: Hai Van Pass by private car or motorbike (the Top Gear road) — this is the most photogenic day of the trip. Day 4–6: Hoi An. Ancient town, lantern boats, tailors who will make you a suit in 24 hours for ₹7,000, An Bang beach 15 minutes away by bicycle (rent 30,000 VND/day). Day 7: fly Da Nang back to Hanoi or straight home via HCMC.

What things cost (October 2026)

Street pho: 40–60,000 VND (₹135–200). A proper restaurant dinner for two: 500,000 VND (₹1,700). Hanoi Old Quarter hotel: ₹2,500–4,000/night. Hoi An homestay with pool: ₹3,500. Domestic flight Hanoi–Da Nang: ₹3,000–4,500. Grab bike rides across the city: 25,000 VND (₹85). Seven days, all-in, excluding international flights: ₹20,000–25,000. With flights from India (₹16,000–22,000 return via Kolkata/Delhi/Mumbai): ₹40,000 total. Vietnam is the cheapest international trip an Indian can currently take that doesn't feel cheap.

Three first-timer mistakes

1. Crossing the street like it has rules. Hanoi traffic is a river — walk at a steady pace, no sudden stops, and let bikes flow around you. It looks like chaos; it's actually a system. 2. Renting a motorbike without an IDP. Vietnamese police fine foreigners regularly — 400,000+ VND for no international driving permit. Get the IDP from your local RTO in India before flying. 3. Only doing Hanoi and Saigon. The middle — Hue, Hai Van, Hoi An — is the country. Skipping it to save one travel day is the classic error.

The food, honestly

Pho is the gateway, not the destination. Bun cha (Hanoi), banh mi (anywhere, 25,000 VND), cao lau (only in Hoi An), com tam (Saigon). Rule of thumb: the plastic-stool place with no English menu and a queue of locals is the best meal of your day, every day.

FAQ

Is Vietnam safe for Indian tourists? Very. Petty scams exist (taxi meters, shoe-shine tricks) but violent crime against tourists is rare. Grab app works for everything.

Best time to visit? North: October–December. Central (Hoi An): February–May. The whole country at once is never perfect — pick your region's season or accept some rain.

Do they speak English? In tourist zones, enough. Google Translate's camera mode handles menus. Learn "cam on" (thank you) and you'll get better prices everywhere.

How many days are enough? Seven for Hanoi + Hoi An. Ten if you add Saigon and the Mekong. Fourteen if you're doing the full country — north to south by train is a bucket-list run.
