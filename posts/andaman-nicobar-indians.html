Andaman on ₹30,000 is not a luxury claim. It's just what it costs when you skip the seaplane.

By Swami Chavan · Published 5 October 2026 · 9 min read

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are the one tropical destination Indian travellers can fly to without a passport, without a visa, without immigration — and somehow the average package quote is ₹45,000 and starts with the word "luxury." I've been twice. Both trips cost under ₹30,000 per person. The difference between ₹30,000 and ₹50,000 is mostly the seaplane, the resort in Havelock you don't need, and the AC ferry a travel agent sold you. Here's the trip that costs what it should.

The permit: 5 minutes, free, at the airport

Indian nationals don't need a permit for the Andamans. Foreigners get a free Restricted Area Permit on arrival at Port Blair airport. You just walk out. Your domestic ID works. No passport needed.

Day 1: Port Blair — Cellular Jail and Corbyn's Cove

Land at Veer Savarkar Airport (VSK). Return flights from Chennai or Kolkata: ₹8,000-13,000 in October. From Delhi, connect through Chennai — total ₹12,000-15,000. Stay in Port Blair one night — Hotel Sentinel or Sea Princess, both ₹1,800-2,500/night including breakfast. Don't book beach-side resorts in Port Blair; you're leaving the island the next morning.

Afternoon: the Cellular Jail (₹50 entry, light and sound show ₹100, book at the counter). The sound-and-light is at 5:30 PM (Hindi) and 6:30 PM (English). Do it. Not because it's patriotic — because it's the one thing in the Andamans that tells you why the islands exist in the Indian imagination. Skip it and you're just on a beach.

Evening: Corbyn's Cove — 8 km from the city, ₹200 auto-rickshaw. A small curve of beach with war bunkers from Japanese occupation. Quick visit, back by dark.

Day 2: Havelock — the government ferry vs the Makruzz

From Phoenix Bay Jetty, two ferry options: government ferry (₹300-400, 2.5 hours, basic) or Makruzz / Green Ocean (₹1,200-1,500, 1.5 hours, AC). The government ferry is slower but has one thing the others don't: a deck where you can sit outside and watch the Andaman Sea. The Makruz is for people who want to arrive in air conditioning. Take the government ferry in the morning (9 AM), Makruz if you booked late. Book at the official counter: andamantourism.gov.in. Do not pay agents ₹500 extra "booking fees" — they buy at the counter and resell.

On Havelock (now officially Swaraj Dweep): stay at Beach No. 5 (Vijaynagar) for guesthouses ₹1,500-2,500/night. Beach No. 3 has the resorts (₹5,000+). You want Beach No. 5 — the same beach, the same sunset, one-third the price.

Day 3: Radhanagar Beach — go at 2 PM, not sunset

Radhanagar is the beach that got Time magazine's "Best Beach in Asia" tag. The photos are real. The crowd strategy matters: every tourist goes at 4:30 PM for sunset. If you go at 2 PM, you share the beach with 10-15 people, the light is better for photography, and you can actually swim in the designated safe zone (the shore current at evening is stronger). Entry: ₹50. The beach closes at 5 PM.

Contrarian call: skip Elephant Beach. It's a half-day boat trip for ₹1,200/person with snorkeling, but the corals there have been declining for five years — the operator boats damage the reef every day. Instead, snorkel at South Button or Aquarium — less restricted but better coral when the operator boats aren't there. Discuss with a local fisherman at the jetty, not the resort desk.

Day 4: Neil Island (Shaheed Dweep) — the one to slow down on

Neil is one hour from Havelock by government ferry (₹300). 10 square km. Three beaches — Bharatpur (for snorkeling), Laxmanpur (for sunset and the natural bridge rock formation), and Sitapur (sunrise beach). Rent a scooty on Neil — ₹400/day. There are two roads on the whole island. Stay at Pearl Park Beach Resort (₹2,000/night) or one of the homestays opening up (₹1,200-1,500). One full day on Neil is enough; two is perfect.

Natural Bridge: a 20-minute walk west from Laxmanpur along the rocks at low tide — check the tide timing with locals. Do NOT cross the bridge; it's a natural sea arch, not a walking path.

Day 5: Back to Port Blair — Ross Island and Chidiya Tapu

Government ferry back from Neil to Port Blair: ₹300, 1.5 hours, once daily (typically 9 AM). On Port Blair, do two things: (1) Ross Island (formerly Netaji Subhas Island) — 10 min boat from Aberdeen Jetty, ₹300 boat + ₹50 entry — the abandoned British administrative capital, reclaimed by forest and deer. Worth 2 hours. (2) Chidiya Tapu sunset — 25 km from Port Blair, the one sunset spot locals use. ₹500 auto. Skip the tourist-sunset at Corbyn's Cove; go to Chidiya Tapu.

What it actually costs (October 2026)

5 days, two people, budget-mid: return flights (Delhi-Port Blair-Via-Chennai) ₹27,000 for two, stay ₹1,800/night average per room = ₹9,000 for 5 nights, ferries ₹2,200 total, food ₹800/day for two = ₹4,000, beach entries + scooty + transport ₹3,500. Total: ₹45,700 for two people, ₹23,000 per person. Solo: add ₹4,500 for single-occupancy rooms.

The agent package you'll be quoted: ₹38,000 per person for 5 days/4 nights — but it's 4 nights, not 5 days, and includes the resort they get a commission on and the Makruz. The fractal math: the same trip, cheaper, if you do it yourself.

Three mistakes every Indian Andaman first-timer makes

1. Booking Havelock resort for the full stay. Two nights on Havelock, one on Neil, one in Port Blair. Three nights at one resort wastes Neil Island entirely. 2. Monsoon season. May to October is monsoon — ferries cancel, water is rough, visibility underwater is poor. Go November to April. October is the edge of acceptable — you get some rain but also off-season rates. 3. The "island hopping" package. Six islands in 5 days is a tourist conveyor. Two islands (Havelock, Neil) for 5 days is the actual trip.

FAQ

Do I need a passport for the Andamans? No. Indian nationals fly domestic with any government ID (Aadhaar, voter ID, PAN). Foreign nationals get a free Restricted Area Permit on arrival.

Is October a good month to go? Borderline. The monsoon is ending but rough seas can still affect ferries. November to February is the prime window. October if you want lower prices and fewer tourists.

Can I drink on the beaches? Andaman is a union territory. Public drinking on beaches is prohibited and enforced. Drink at your hotel or designated licensed spots.

What about scuba diving? Havelock has PADI centres — ₹3,500-5,000 for a shore dive for beginners. Always verify the operator is PADI-certified (Blue Planet, Dive India, Barefoot). The one area where the budget option is more dangerous than the mid-range option.
