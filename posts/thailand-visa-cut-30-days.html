Thailand slashed visa-free entry from 60 to 30 days. Here's what actually changed for Indian travellers.

By Swami Chavan · Published 5 October 2026 · 6 min read

Until September 14, 2026, Indian passport holders could enter Thailand visa-free and stay up to 60 days. No paperwork, no fee, no question. Then Thailand quietly reformed the whole scheme — 21 countries saw their visa-free window cut, and Indians went from 60 days to 30. The government called it a "border tightening." Travel agents called it a shock. Neither is the full story. Here's what actually changed, what it means for your trip, and whether you should care.

The new rule in plain language

As of September 15, 2026, Indian passport holders get 30 days visa-free entry to Thailand for tourism — down from 60. The entry itself is still free (no visa fee). You still don't need to apply for anything before you travel. The change is purely about duration of stay.

If you planned to stay more than 30 days, you now have three options: (1) exit and re-enter for another 30-day stamp (a visa run), (2) apply for a Tourist Visa (TR) at a Thai embassy in India before departure, which gives 60 days, or (3) apply for a Visa on Arrival at the airport, which is 15 days and costs 2,200 THB (~₹5,500). For most holiday trips under two weeks, the new rule changes absolutely nothing.

Who this actually affects

If you're flying to Bangkok for five days, Phuket for a week, or a Pattaya weekend — you're unaffected. Your 30 visa-free days cover the trip. This change hits exactly one category: the long-stay digital nomad or the "work-from-Thailand for two months" traveller who was using the 60-day stamp to skip a tourist visa. That loophole is closed.

Contrarian call: if you genuinely need 60+ days, get the Tourist Visa in India. It costs ₹2,500 at VFS Global takes 3-5 working days for Indian residents, and Bangkok is the wrong place to discover you're overstaying.

Overstaying: what it costs now

Thailand charges 500 THB per day of overstay (~₹1,250/day), capped at 20,000 THB (~₹50,000). The fine is paid at immigration on departure. But here's the detail that matters: if you overstay and get caught at a checkpoint rather than at the airport, you can be detained, blacklisted, and deported — the fine becomes the least of your problems. There is no grace period. If your stamp says October 30 and you leave October 31, you pay one day's fine. If immigration finds you on the street on October 31, you might not make it to the airport yourself.

The TDAC: a new entry form nobody mentions

Alongside the visa window reform, Thailand introduced the Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) — a mandatory online form you must complete before arrival or departure. It replaced the old paper TM6 card at major airports. You fill it out online (free) up to 72 hours before your flight. If you skip it, your airline may deny boarding. Most airlines briefly remind passengers, but a significant number of Indian travellers have been held up at check-in counters in Delhi and Mumbai because the system is new and awareness is low. Complete it at the official portal: tdac.immigration.go.th. No third-party site. No fee.

30-day visa-free vs 15-day VOA: which to pick

For trips under 30 days, the visa-free entry is free and automatic. For trips under 15 days where you couldn't plan ahead, VOA at the airport costs 2,200 THB and covers 15 days. The visa-free option is strictly better for any Indian traveller on a holiday of up to a month — use it, don't overthink it.

Updated visa summary for Indians (October 2026)

30 days visa-free (no fee, no application): tourism only, 30 days per entry, extendable once for 30 more days at an immigration office in Thailand for 1,900 THB. Official reference: immigration.go.th.

15 days Visa on Arrival: 2,200 THB, available at 42 designated checkpoints including Suvarnabhumi, Don Mueang, Phuket, and Chiang Mai airports. 6-month valid passport, return ticket, and proof of accommodation required.

60-day Tourist Visa (TR): apply at Thai embassy/VFS in India. Single entry ₹2,500 or multiple entry ₹5,000. Most reliable option for longer trips.

FAQ

I already booked a trip with a 45-day stay. What do I do? Two options: apply for a 60-day Tourist Visa in India before departure (fastest, ₹2,500, 3-5 working days), or enter on visa-free 30 days and extend at a Thai immigration office for 30 more days for 1,900 THB (~₹4,750). Do not overstay.

Is the visa-free entry still free? Yes. No visa fee, no application. You just show up with a valid passport and return ticket.

Does this affect Phuket, Krabi, or Koh Samui differently? No. The 30-day rule applies nationwide. Airport of entry doesn't matter.

Can I do a visa run to Laos or Malaysia and get another 30 days? Technically yes. Thai immigration is now more likely to question repeated back-to-back 30-day entries. If you're genuinely a tourist continuing your trip, keep your boarding passes and hotel bookings. If you're living in Thailand on 30-day stamps, expect problems eventually.
