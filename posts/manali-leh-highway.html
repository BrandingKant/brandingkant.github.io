The Manali-Leh highway is the road trip Indian travellers talk about most and prepare for least.

By Swami Chavan · Published 5 October 2026 · 9 min read

The Manali-Leh highway is 428 km of one of the highest motorable roads on earth. Five passes above 15,000 feet. Two Himalayan ranges crossed. It closes every year from October/November to May because of snow — and reopens to everyone who Googled "road trip to Ladakh" the week before. The people who do it right are not the brave ones. They're the ones who checked the weather, serviced the vehicle, and read about altitude sickness more than once. Here's the full plan.

The season — narrow, real, non-negotiable

The Manali-Leh highway typically opens in late May or early June and closes by mid-October. The exact dates change yearly based on snow clearance by BRO (Border Roads Organisation). In 2026, the highway opened on May 28 and was projected to close by October 15 based on weather forecasts. You can check current status at bro.gov.in or the Ladakh police travel advisories. Do not plan this trip in October unless you're willing to risk being stranded — the first snowfall closes the passes and they don't reopen until spring.

The best window: mid-June to mid-September. July and August are the monsoon — rain on the Manali side, generally dry once you cross Rohtang. August has the best weather but the highest traffic. June is the prettiest (snow still on the passes) and the riskiest (avalanche remnants on the road). September is the sweet spot — stable weather, dropping tourist numbers, leaves turning on the Leh side.

Day 1: Manali to Jispa — acclimatize before you commit

Manali (6,700 ft). Spend two days here minimum — not for Manali, for acclimatization. Old Manali cafes, the Hadimba Temple, the Mall Road. The mistake people make: land in Manali from Delhi (flight to Bhuntar, or overnight Volvo from Delhi ₹1,200-1,800, 12 hours) and leave for Leh the next morning. That's the classic altitude sickness setup.

On Day 3, drive Manali to Jispa (3,200m / 10,500 ft) — 60 km, 3 hours including Rohtang Pass (3,978m / 13,050 ft). Stop at Marhi before Rohtang for a 30-min break — let the body adjust. At Jispa, stay overnight. Guesthouse ₹1,000-1,500. Don't push to Sarchu on Day 1 — Sarchu is at 14,000 ft and you will not sleep.

Day 2: Jispa to Leh — the long day

This is the defining day. 330 km, 12-14 hours including the three major passes: Baralacha La (4,890m / 16,040 ft), Nakee La (4,740m / 15,550 ft), and Tanglang La (5,328m / 17,480 ft — the second-highest motorable pass in the world after Khardung La).

Leave Jispa at 5 AM. Stop at Sarchu (14,000 ft) for brunch — 30 minutes, no longer. The BP check: if anyone feels dizzy or nauseous here, descend, not ascend. The Deepak Tal lake near Baralacha is a 2-minute stop. The Morey Plains — 40 km of flat high-altitude plateau at 15,500 ft — are surreal and surrealism means head out, photos only. Don't spend long; the altitude here is punishing.

Tanglang La is the test. If the weather is clear, stop 10 minutes at the top — the prayer flags, the view. If there's snow or wind at the top, don't stop — descend immediately. By evening (6-7 PM), arrive in Leh. Sleep in Leh — you've earned the altitude badge.

Day 3: Leh — rest day, not optional

One full day in Leh (11,500 ft). Do not go to Pangong, Nubra, or Hanle on Day 3. Walk around Leh market, eat at the German Bakery, drink butter tea (₹50), visit Shanti Stupa and Leh Palace. Hydrate (4L water/day). The body needs this day. If you push to Pangong Lake on Day 3 after arriving at midnight, you will get altitude sickness — almost guaranteed.

Day 4: Pangong Lake or Nubra Valley — pick one

Pangong: 5 hours from Leh, 14,500 ft. The 134 km blue lake across India and China. Stay overnight at a homestay in Spangmik or Man a village (₹1,000-1,500 including dinner). Return next day. The road crosses Chang La (17,586 ft) — one of the highest pass crossings to a lake. Take it slow.

Nubra Valley: 5 hours from Leh, 10,000 ft — lower than Leh. Crossing Khardung La (17,982 ft / 5,359m) — one of the highest motorable roads in the world (signposted). Sand dunes at Hunder, double-humped Bactrian camels (₹200 for a short ride), Diskit Monastery. Stay at Hunder (₹1,500-2,000). Nubra is easier than Pangong on altitude — go here if you have time for only one.

Altitude sickness: the real risk nobody takes seriously

Acute Mountain Sickness (AMS) kills people on this route every year. The symptoms: headache (persistent, not relieved by paracetamol), nausea, loss of appetite, insomnia (not just wakeful, true inability to sleep), confusion. The progression to HAPE (High Altitude Pulmonary Edema) or HACE (High Altitude Cerebral Edema) is the killer — shortness of breath at rest, inability to walk straight, severe confusion. If any of these appear: descend immediately. Not tomorrow, not after one hour of observation — now. Every hour higher is an hour worse.

Prevention: diamox (acetazolamide) 125 mg twice daily starting one day before the ascent — prescription medication, talk to your doctor. Hydration (4-5L/day). No alcohol on the highway. Light food. Sleep at the lowest altitude you can reach each day.

Contrarian call: if you have any chronic condition (BP, heart, asthma), fly to Leh instead of driving. The flight puts you at 11,500 ft in one hour — altitude sickness can still hit, but the controlled descent of a road trip over multiple passes is genuinely more dangerous than the controlled altitude of a flight.

Vehicle or shared taxi — what actually works

Self-drive: your own SUV or rented from Manali (Innova ₹3,500-5,000/day, Royal Enfield ₹1,800/day). You need high clearance and know how to handle water crossings. Do not take a sedan. Do not take a bike you've never ridden in the mountains.

Shared taxi (Innova from Manali to Leh, 7-9 people, ₹2,000-2,500 per seat). Book in Manali, leaves early morning, reaches Leh same night. The shared taxi is faster and you sleep through the passes — but you don't stop for photos.

Bus: HPTDC runs a luxury bus Manali-Leh (₹2,500-3,000), 2 days with overnight at Keylong. Slower, more comfortable, less control.

What it actually costs (September 2026)

Manali to Leh to Pangong to Nubra to Leh, fly back to Delhi: 7 days. Delhi-Manali Volvo ₹1,500. Shared taxi variants ₹4,500 total (Manali-Leh, Leh-Pangong return, Leh-Nubra return). Stays ₹1,500/night average = ₹9,000 for 6 nights. Food ₹600/day = ₹4,200. Permits (Inner Line for Pangong, Nubra, Hanle — ₹100 each, environment fee ₹400) = ₹600. Leh-Delhi flight ₹5,000-8,000. Total: ₹25,000-30,000 per person. Self-driving adds vehicle rental and fuel; subtract shared taxi costs.

FAQ

Can I do this trip in 4 days? No, not safely. Minimum 7 days including 2 acclimatization days. Anyone selling you a 4-day package is selling altitude sickness.

Is the road open in October? Sometimes. The first week, maybe. After October 15 — unlikely. Don't plan this trip for October. Plan for June-September.

Do I need an Inner Line Permit? Yes for Pangong, Nubra, Hanle, Turtuk, and other border areas. Available at the DC office in Leh (₹100 each) or online at lahdnlehpolic.in. Indian citizens — bring passport or voter ID and 4 photos. Foreign nationals: Protected Area Permit, different process.

Mobile network? BSNL postpaid works in Leh, Nubra, Pangong. Jio and Airtel have partial coverage in Leh town and some stretches. Prepaid from outside Ladakh does NOT work — only postpaid. Buy or convert to a postpaid SIM before you go.
