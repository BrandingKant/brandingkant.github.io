Wi-Fi 6 Router for an Indian Home: What to Buy, Where to Place It, and Why Your Speed Test Lies

By Swami Chavan·Published 2026-11-04·7 min read

The most common Indian home networking problem is not bandwidth, it is geometry. A 300Mbps connection delivering 40Mbps in the bedroom is not a slow connection — it is a signal that had to pass through two walls and a fridge. Buying a bigger router rarely fixes geometry.

Fix the location before you buy anything

  • Place the router centrally and high — shelf height, not floor level.
  • Never inside a cupboard, behind a TV or next to a microwave.
  • Keep it away from metal: almirahs, grills, and mirrored walls reflect signal.
  • Point antennas vertically for same-floor coverage.
  • Test with a speed test in each room before spending a rupee.

What Wi-Fi 6 actually changes

Wi-Fi 6 is not mainly about peak speed. Its real benefit in an Indian home is handling many devices at once without stutter: phones, TVs, cameras, smart plugs, laptops. If your home has fifteen or more connected devices, Wi-Fi 6 improves the experience of all of them. If you have four devices and a 100Mbps line, a good Wi-Fi 5 router will do.

Mesh versus extender versus repeater

A repeater halves your speed at the extension point and keeps the same network name, which confuses devices. A mesh system hands devices between units properly and keeps one network name with full speed on each node. For a single dead zone in a 2BHK, a two-unit mesh is worth the extra cost over a repeater.

The speed test lie

Speed tests measure the fastest possible moment on a single device. Real usage is multiple devices, sustained, through walls. Judge your network by video calls that do not freeze and downloads that finish predictably, not by a number on a test screen.

The verdict

Move the router first, then measure. Buy a single Wi-Fi 6 router for a small flat with a central location. Buy a two-unit mesh the moment there is a dead zone you actually use. Never buy a repeater expecting mesh performance.

Quick answers

Do I need a Wi-Fi 6 router for a 100Mbps connection?

Not for speed — a good Wi-Fi 5 router will saturate 100Mbps. Wi-Fi 6 helps when many devices are connected at once, or when you later upgrade to a faster line.

Is mesh better than a range extender?

Yes, for coverage. Mesh keeps one network name, hands devices between nodes cleanly and maintains speed better. An extender is cheaper but halves throughput at the extension point.

Does router placement really matter more than the router?

In most homes, yes. Moving a router from a corner cupboard to a central shelf routinely improves bedroom signal more than upgrading the hardware.
