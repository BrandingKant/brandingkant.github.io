Nepal for Indians is the easiest international trip in the world. That's not a marketing line — it's the rules.

By Swami Chavan · Published 5 October 2026 · 8 min read

No visa. No e-visa. No embassy. No permit (Kathmandu, Pokhara, Chitwan). Your Indian rupees work in shops. Your Aadhaar works at the border. You can fly or cross by land from four states. Nepal is the only international destination where Indians genuinely travel as if it's domestic — and it costs like domestic too. A 7-day Kathmandu-Pokhara circuit from Delhi is ₹20,000-28,000 all-in. The mountains are real. Here's the plan.

The entry: four ways, pick the prettiest

1. Fly: Delhi-Kathmandu from ₹6,000-9,000 return (Buddha Air, IndiGo, Air India). 1.5 hours. Most practical. 2. Fly to Pokhara directly: Buddha Air from Delhi directly to Pokhara exists seasonally — skips the Kathmandu-Pokhara road entirely. Check schedules. 3. Land border, Sunauli (from Varanasi/Gorakhpur): bus to the border, cross on foot, local bus to Kathmandu (8 hours). Total cost under ₹3,000. 4. Land border, Raxaul (from Patna): closest to Kathmandu by road. Shared jeep to Kathmandu in 8 hours, ₹1,500. The land routes are the cheapest option in the world for a mountain trip, but they are long. For first-timers, fly.

Indian citizens do not need a visa. At the airport, you walk through the Indian national lane — show any Indian ID, get your passport stamped (optional; recommended for peace of mind). Indian currency (₹100 and ₹500 denominations) is widely accepted in Nepal. Do not bring ₹1,000 or ₹2,000 notes — they are not accepted per Nepal's regulation, and exchanging them means losing on the rate.

Day 1-2: Kathmandu — the valleys, not the thamel tourist circuit

Stay in Thamel for one night if you want convenience and noise. Then move to Boudha or Patan — quieter, less tourist markup, ₹1,200-1,800/night guesthouses. In Boudha, the stupa is a 2-minute walk and you'll hear morning chants at 5 AM.

Day 2: the Kathmandu Valley UNESCO circuit — Boudhanath Stupa (free, donations welcome), Pashupatinath (₹1,000 entry for foreigners, free for Indians — show your ID), Swayambhunath (the monkey temple, ₹200 for Indians). These three can be done in a day by taxi (₹2,500 full day) or by local bus (₹50 per ride). Patan Durbar Square (₹1,000 entry, ₹250 for Indians) is worth the afternoon — the museum alone justifies the trip.

Contrarian call: skip Kathmandu Durbar Square. Reconstruction after the 2015 quake is still in progress. The royal palace section is largely closed. Boudha and Patan give you more for less hassle.

Day 3-4: Pokhara — the lake and the mountains you came for

Kathmandu to Pokhara: tourist bus ₹700-1,000 (7 hours, departure 7 AM), flight ₹4,000-6,000 (25 minutes). The bus is the better experience — the Prithvi Highway follows the Trishuli River, and you will see the Himalaya appear for the first time. Sit on the right side.

Stay in Lakeside Pokhara — guesthouses ₹1,200-2,000/night. Rent a scooty (₹500/day) and do: Devi's Fall + Gupteshwor Cave (combined ticket ₹100 for Indians), International Mountain Museum (₹500 for Indians — worth it), and the World Peace Pagoda (free — 45 min hike or ₹700 boat across the lake). Sunrise at Sarangkot — the viewpoint above Pokhara for Annapurna sunrise. Leave at 4:30 AM, shared jeep ₹500 PP. Bring a thermos of tea. It's -2C at 6 AM at 5,200 feet.

Day 5: Annapurna view trek — Australian Camp

If you want a taste of trekking without a 14-day Annapurna Circuit, do Australian Camp: 2-hour drive from Pokhara to Kande (₹1,000 taxi), then 3-hour hike up to Australian Camp (6,900 ft). Stay overnight at the teahouse (₹800 room, ₹400-600 dal bhat — pronouns matter: it's called Dal Bhat Tarkari). Sunrise view: Annapurna South and Machapuchare from the same spot. You don't need a guide; the path is well-marked. Bring a headlamp and a power bank for the teahouse (often no electric).

Permit: Indian nationals don't need the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) for short hikes to Australian Camp and Dhampus. If you go further (Poon Hill, Annapurna Base Camp) you do — get it at the Pokhara ACAP office (₹2,000 for foreigners, free for Indians with ID, ₹100 admin for TIMS card).

Day 6: Chitwan — the rhinos and the jungle

Pokhara to Chitwan: tourist bus (₹1,000, 5 hours) or flight (₹4,500, 25 min to Bharatpur airport). Stay in Sauraha — guesthouses ₹1,000-1,500/night. The next morning: jeep safari in Chitwan National Park (₹1,200-2,000 per person in a shared jeep, 3-4 hours). One-horned rhinos, sloth bears, occasionally tigers. The rhino sighting rate is high — the tiger sighting rate is the jungle lottery. Mornings and late afternoons are best for wildlife.

Do the village walk in Sauraha — local Tharu villages, free, more rewarding than the elephant breeding center (which has welfare issues).

Day 7: Back to Kathmandu or exit

Chitwan to Kathmandu: tourist bus (₹800, 6 hours), flight (₹4,500, 30 min). Or — if you entered by land through Sunauli — exit through the same border. Or continue to Lumbini (birthplace of the Buddha, 2 hours from Chitwan) before returning to India.

What it actually costs (October 2026)

7 days, two people, budget-mid: return flights Delhi-Kathmandu ₹15,000 for two, Kathmandu-Pokhara bus ₹1,800, Pokhara-Chitwan bus ₹2,000, stay ₹1,500/night average = ₹10,500 for 7 nights, food ₹800/day for two = ₹5,600, safari + sarangkot + scooty + buses in Pokhara ₹4,000. Total: ₹38,900 for two, ₹19,500 per person. Solo: add ₹4,000 for single rooms.

The three things that catch Indians off guard

1. The ₹2,000 note ban. Indian ₹2,000 notes are not accepted in Nepal. Bring ₹500 denominations — they're widely accepted. Or withdraw Nepali rupees at the airport ATM (Nepali Rs is pegged at 1.6 INR : 1 NPR — the exchange rate is stable but understand it before you buy). 2. The altitude. Pokhara is 2,700 ft — comfortable. Sarangkot is 5,200 ft. Australian Camp is 6,900 ft. Annapurna Base Camp is 13,500 ft. Unless you're trekking deeper, altitude is not a major risk on this route. Hydrate, rest, headlamp. 3. The dirt. Kathmandu air quality is poor in winter — PM2.5 routinely exceeds Delhi. Carry a mask, wear it. Reduced to 3-5x safe limits in autumn, Chitwan is cleaner.

FAQ

Do Indians need a visa or permit for Nepal? No. Under the 1950 Treaty, Indians can live, work, and travel in Nepal freely. Show an Indian ID at the border or airport.

Is Indian money accepted? Yes — ₹100 and ₹500 notes widely. ₹2,000 notes are not. ATMs in Kathmandu and Pokhara dispense Nepali rupees.

Is October/November good to go? The best months — post-monsoon clear skies, visibility in the mountains is at its highest. October is peak. November is second best.

Can I do Everest Base Camp as an Indian? Yes. The flight to Lukla from Kathmandu is ₹5,000-7,000 one-way. The trek is 12 days. Permits (Sagarmatha National Park, TIMS) are free or nominal for Indians. Altitude is a real risk — do not rush the itinerary.
