Dubai from India on a budget is real — ₹45,000 for 4 days. Here's the exact math.

By Swami Chavan · Published 4 October 2026 · 8 min read

Dubai has a branding problem. It's sold to Indians as a luxury flex — Desert Safari in a Land Cruiser, Burj Khalifa fine dining, a hotel on the Palm. And then you land and find out the metro costs 4 dirhams, the beach is free, and the best shawarma of your life is 12 AED. I've done Dubai three ways: blowout, mid, and budget. The budget trip was the best one. Here's the full breakdown.

Getting there and the visa

Flights: Mumbai to Dubai return on a low-cost carrier (flydubai, Air India Express, IndiGo) runs ₹18,000–25,000 if you book 6–8 weeks out. Book Tuesday flights, skip Thursday–Sunday. Visa: Indians with a valid US visa, green card, or UK/EU residence get visa on arrival. Everyone else needs a pre-arranged UAE visa — done through airlines or tour operators in 3–4 working days, roughly ₹6,500–7,500 for 30 days single entry. Emirates, flydubai, and Air India all arrange it if you fly with them. Track it yourself at the official portal, not through a WhatsApp agent.

Sleep cheap, sleep smart

Deira and Al Barsha have clean 3-star doubles at ₹4,000–5,500/night, both on the metro line. Downtown Dubai for the same room is triple. You don't need a view of the Burj from bed — you need a 10-minute metro hop to it. Two stops from Al Barsha station is Mall of the Emirates. That's the whole strategy.

The free Dubai (yes, it exists)

JBR Beach, Kite Beach, Sunset Beach — all free, all excellent, and Sunset Beach has the Burj Al Arab in your photo for zero dirhams. The Old Dubai walk — Al Fahidi historical district, the textile souk, the abra crossing — is the best two hours in the city and costs 1 AED (₹23) for the boat. Global Village in season (Oct–Apr) is 27 AED entry and worth an entire evening. Contrarian call: skip the Burj Khalifa "At the Top" (₹3,800–5,000+). Instead, go to the Dubai Mall fountain show — free, every 30 minutes from 6 PM — and have a mint lemonade (18 AED) while the Burj does the work above you. If you must go up, book the 154th floor lounge instead of "At the Top" — comparable price, seated, unlimited drinks, and you're not cattle-penned at the glass for 15 minutes.

What's worth paying for

Desert safari with BBQ dinner: 150–250 AED (₹3,400–5,700) through a local operator, not the hotel concierge. Dhow dinner cruise at Dubai Marina: 100–150 AED. Metro day pass: 20 AED for unlimited red-line rides. Museum of the Future: 145 AED — book a week ahead or don't bother.

Real 4-day budget (per person, October 2026)

Flights ₹20,000 · visa ₹7,000 · stay ₹8,400 (3 nights shared double at Al Barsha) · Nol metro card + abras ₹800 · food ₹3,600 (mix of Ravi Restaurant, karak chai, mall food courts — a full plate at Ravi is 15 AED) · desert safari ₹4,500 · fountain + free beaches ₹0 · total: about ₹44,000–47,000. Double it for the Instagram Dubai.

The three traps

1. Taxis from DXB at 3 AM — 100+ AED. Take the metro (5 AED red line to Al Barsha) or book a Careem in advance. 2. "Free" Dubai tour offers — they're shopping-mall shuttles with commission stops. The gold souk is free to wander by metro (all stops on the green line). 3. Exchanging at the airport. The best rates in the city are at the Al Fahidi souk exchanges; the airport gives you the worst spread in the UAE.

FAQ

How many days are enough for Dubai? Three to four. It's a small city with an aggressive highway system — you can see the highlights without burning a week.

Is Dubai expensive for Indian tourists? Only if you do the Instagram version. The metro-and-beach version costs about the same as a Goa trip done lazily.

Best time to visit? November to March. October is bearable, June–August is 45°C — the locals don't go outside; neither should you.

Can I drink the tap water? No. Bottled water is 1–2 AED everywhere. That's not a trap, just geology.
