Bhutan for Indians: the only place you can't overplan, and the math for 2026

By Swami Chavan · Published 4 October 2026 · 8 min read

Bhutan is the trip every Indian traveller has on the list and postpones forever — it feels expensive, complicated, and vaguely rationed. Here's the reality after three trips: for Indians, it's the easiest international border in Asia, the SDF is the only real cost, and the country actively rewards people who arrive with fewer plans. The guide below is the 5-day version that works.

The India advantage

Indians don't need a visa — just a permit: entry processing at the border (Phuentsholing) or online permit pre-approval, passport or voter ID both accepted. The Sustainable Development Fee for Indians is ₹1,200/night — what everyone else pays is many multiples. Children under 6 are exempt, 6–12 half. This is the best-value cross-border deal India has.

The route that works (5 days)

Day 1: Fly Paro (IndiGo/Druk Air from Delhi, Kolkata, or Bagdogra) or enter by road via Phuentsholing (Jaigaon, West Bengal — the border is literally a gate). The road entry is an experience itself: Bhutan switches on within one street. Day 2: Thimphu — Buddha Point, the weekend market if your dates land right, and momos that ruin all other momos. Day 3: The drive to Punakha over Dochu La pass — 108 chortens in the clouds; the Punakha Dzong at the river confluence is Bhutan's most beautiful building. Day 4: Back west via Paro — Paro Dzong, the national museum, and archery practice happening anywhere. Day 5: Tiger's Nest. Start 7 AM. 4–5 hours round trip, 900 metres up, and a monastery on a cliff face that no photo has ever done justice. Tea house at the halfway viewpoint — the lasagna at a Buddhist monastery halfway up a Himalayan cliff is a real menu item and it's honestly good.

What things cost (October 2026)

SDF ₹1,200/night. Hotels in Thimphu/Paro: ₹2,500–6,000/night. Shared taxis and buses: ₹100–400 inter-city. Meals: 150–400 BTN (₹150–400 — the ngultrum is pegged 1:1 to the rupee, no forex drama). The 5-day trip by road: ₹18,000–25,000 per person. By air add ₹9,000–15,000. Bhutan costs roughly what a good Himachal trip costs — which is the quiet scandal nobody mentions.

Three things to know

1. No traffic lights in the country. Thimphu has a policeman conducting a roundabout with hand signals, and he's a minor celebrity. Adjust your pace accordingly. 2. It's a smoke-free, plastic-free country — fines are real, enforcement is polite, don't be the guy with the cigarette. 3. March–May and September–November are the windows. October has the Thimphu Tshechu festival — book hotels 3+ months ahead or camp in someone's kindness.

FAQ

Do Indians need a passport? Passport OR voter ID both work at the border. Aadhaar/PAN are not travel documents here — carry the right one.

Is Bhutan expensive? Not for Indians. The ₹1,200/night SDF plus normal travel costs make it comparable to domestic hill trips — with a visa-free stamp in the story.

How many days are enough? Five for Paro–Thimphu–Punakha. Seven to add Bumthang (the cultural heartland). Do not rush Tiger's Nest into an afternoon.

Is Tiger's Nest hard? Moderate — it's steady uphill but a paved trail with horses available to the halfway point. Anyone who does a morning gym class can do it.
