Japan under ₹1 lakh from India is not a lie. It's the math nobody bothers to do.

By Swami Chavan · Published 5 October 2026 · 10 min read

"Japan is too expensive" is the laziest travel take of 2026. The truth: Japan's yen has weakened against the rupee to where a Tokyo meal costs less than a Mumbai meal. The visa for Indians got simpler — eVisa since 2024, ₹500 flat. The JR Rail Pass went up in price in 2023 but the individual tickets are still cheap for one-city and two-city trips. The seven-day budget from Delhi to Tokyo to Kyoto and back is ₹75,000-95,000 if you eat at conbini and sleep in business hotels. Here's how the math actually breaks down.

The eVisa — what changed and what it costs

As of April 2024, Indian nationals can apply for a Japan eVisa (single-entry, tourism, 90 days) through VFS Global. The fee is ₹500 (flat) — not the ₹3,500 old paper visa. The catch: Japan raised visa fees significantly in July 2026 for most other nationalities to ¥30,000 (~₹17,600), but Indian nationals pay a reduced rate under a bilateral arrangement — confirm the current fee on the embassy site: in.emb-japan.go.jp. Apply 1-3 months before travel. The eVisa is issued as an electronic document — you show it on arrival.

Documents: passport (6+ months validity), photo (45x35mm white background), bank statements (6 months, balance ₹1,50,000+), ITR (last 2-3 years), salary slips (3 months) or business proof, leave letter, itinerary (dates and cities), flight reservation, hotel reservation, cover letter. Same architecture as Schengen.

Day 1-3: Tokyo — the city that nobody prepares you for

Land at Narita (NRT) or Haneda (HND). Narita is 90 min from Tokyo by Narita Express (¥2,800, ~₹1,500). Haneda is 30 min by Keikyu Line (¥600, ~₹330). If your flight from India lands at Haneda, you save an hour and ₹1,200. IndiGo, Air India, ANA, and Japan Airlines all fly from Delhi/Mumbai/Chennai to Narita or Haneda.

Stay in a business hotel — APA Hotel, Sotetsu Fresa, or Dormy Inn. Central Tokyo locations (Shinjuku, Ueno, Asakusa) ₹4,000-6,000/night for a small clean double room. Business hotels are small (15-18 sqm) but spotless. They are not hostels. They are the Japanese budget option.

Day 2: Shibuya Crossing, Yoyogi Park, Harajuku, the Meiji Shrine (free). Walk. Tokyo is a walking city. Day 3: Sensoji Temple in Asakusa (free), Ueno Park (free), Tokyo National Museum (¥1,000, ~₹550), teamLab Planets in Toyosu (¥3,800, ~₹2,100 — book ahead, it's the one experience in Tokyo that sells out).

Day 4-5: Kyoto — the old Japan

Shinkansen (Nozomi) from Tokyo to Kyoto: ¥13,000 one-way (~₹7,200) in 2 hours 15 minutes. The 7-day JR Pass (¥50,000, ~₹27,700) covers this and other JR routes — it's worth it if you're also going to Osaka and Hiroshima. If just Tokyo-Kyoto-Osaka, individual tickets are cheaper than the pass.

Stay in Kyoto central — guesthouses and business hotels ₹3,000-5,000/night. Day 1: Kinkakuji (Golden Pavilion, ¥500), Ryoanji (¥600), Arashiyama Bamboo Grove (free) and the Iwatayama monkey park on the hill above it (¥600 — and yes, you'll come face to face with macaques). Day 2: Fushimi Inari (free — the torii tunnel, go at 7 AM to avoid crowds), Kiyomizu-dera (¥400), walk the Sannenzaka and Ninenzaka preserved streets to Yasaka Shrine (free).

Contrarian call: skip Nara. The deer are lovely. The temple is historic. But every tourist hits Nara, and you can see the same buildings with half the crowds at Horyuji in Ikaruga (¥1,500 by train from Kyoto, ¥600 temple entry).

Day 6: Osaka — food, not castles

Special Rapid train from Kyoto to Osaka: ¥570 (~₹320) in 30 minutes. Osaka is the eating city — okonomiyaki, takoyaki, kushikatsu. Dotonbori at night (free), Tsutenkaku tower (¥800), Shinsekai district. Osaka Castle (¥600 but the interior is a modern museum, not original). Skip the castle, do the food.

Get to Osaka's Kuromon Market at 10 AM — it's a covered food market, ¥300-800 per bite. Octopus, wagyu beef, taiyaki. A full lunch grazing the market is ¥2,000 (~₹1,100) and you eat better than any restaurant.

Day 7: Back to Tokyo, fly out

Shinkansen Osaka to Tokyo: ¥14,000 (~₹7,800), 2.5 hours. Fly out of Haneda if possible — closer to the city.

What it actually costs (October 2026)

Return flights Delhi-Tokyo: ₹40,000-55,000 (IndiGo, Air India). ANA and JAL from ₹55,000. Visa: ₹500. Travel insurance: ₹1,500. JR individual tickets Tokyo-Kyoto-Osaka-Tokyo: ₹16,500. Hotels: 6 nights at ₹4,500 average = ₹27,000. Food: ¥2,500/day at conbini + cheap restaurants = ₹7,200 for 7 days. activities + temples: ₹4,000. SIM (eSIM from Airalo): ₹1,200.

Total: ₹97,900 per person, mid-budget. The solo traveller pays the same hotel cost (business hotels charge per room, not per person, but a single room is sometimes less than a double). Two people split the room.

Absolutely possible under ₹1 lakh. Add ₹15,000 for a nicer hotel level. Subtract ₹5,000 if you fly IndiGo.

The three things that cost more than expected

1. Coffee. A cup of coffee in Tokyo is ¥500-600 (~₹280-330) — at a cafe. Conbini (Family Mart, 7-Eleven) coffee is ¥100-150 (~₹55-85). Drink conbini coffee. It's better than most Indian cafe coffee. 2. Checked luggage on budget airlines. IndiGo includes 15-20kg checked; Air India includes 25kg. Check weight. Over-limit is exorbitant. 3. IC card transit. Get a Suica or Pasmo IC card at a station machine — tap-and-go for all Tokyo subway and JR lines. ¥2,000 average per day for inner-city transit. Cash on card, top up as needed.

What makes Japan different from the expectation

You've been told Japan is crowded. It is — at Shibuya Crossing at 5 PM. Everywhere else, calm and ordered. The Japanese do not jostle. They do not haggle. They do not tip (it's almost rude). The conbini onigiri (rice ball) is ¥120 (~₹65) and will be the best snack you've had. Vending machines are everywhere and they're cheaper than Indian bottlers — ¥150 (~₹85) for 500ml Aquarius.

The language barrier is real but manageable. Google Translate camera mode covers printed menus. Most stations have English signage in Tokyo, Kyoto, Osaka. Smaller cities and rural areas are harder. Carry a data SIM or eSIM — translation uses mobile data.

FAQ

Is Japan safe for solo Indian travellers? Among the safest countries on earth. Violent crime is rare. The real risk is missing a train because you misread the sign.

Do I need a JR Pass? Only if you're doing long-distance travel (Tokyo-Kyoto-Osaka-Hiroshima over 7 days). For one/two-city trips, individual tickets are cheaper.

Can I eat vegetarian in Japan? Hard but possible. Dashi (fish stock) is in almost everything. Look for shojin ryori (Buddhist vegetarian) restaurants — sample at Buddhist temples in Kyoto. Conbini onigiri with rice and umeboshi (ume plum) is safe. Indian restaurants exist in Tokyo and Kyoto (though predictably priced up).

What about cherry blossom and autumn foliage? Cherry blossom: late March to early April. Autumn foliage: mid-November to early December. These are peak seasons for tourists — hotels cost 2x. October is shoulder: cheaper hotels, fewer tourists, mild weather (high 22C, low 14C in Tokyo).
