Kashmir in winter is not a summer postcard. It's quieter, cheaper, and honest about the cold.

By Swami Chavan · Published 5 October 2026 · 9 min read

Summer Kashmir gets all the hype — houseboats on Dal Lake, Gulmarg meadows, Pahalgam valleys. Winter Kashmir is where the tourists leave and the place actually breathes. December to February, temperatures drop to -5C at night in Srinagar and -10C in Gulmarg. Snow covers everything. Dal Lake freezes over in January — houseboats still run but with kangs (fire pots) lit at 4 PM. If you've never sat in a shikara wrapped in a pheran at 2C, you haven't seen the real Kashmir. But you need to know what you're signing up for. Here's the winter plan that works.

Day 1-2: Srinagar — the frozen lake and the old city

Land in Srinagar (flights from Delhi: ₹4,000-7,000 in winter — half the summer rate). Stay on a houseboat in Nigeen Lake — quieter than Dal, better heating, and ₹3,000/night in December vs ₹8,000 in May. The houseboat stove runs on wood and draws dia (hamam) — a room with a heated floor. You will not need a hotel room in Srinagar.

Day 2: shikara ride at 8 AM (₹500 for two hours, not the ₹1,500 they quote summer tourists). Walk the Old City — Maharaj Bazaar, Zaina Kadal, the copper-smith lane. The Jama Masjid is empty and free in winter. The Mughal gardens (Nishat, Shalimar) are snow-covered and the cheapest photos you'll take in Kashmir — zero spectators, ₹24 entry.

Contrarian call: skip Pahalgam in winter. The road closes, Aru Valley is unreachable, and the town is a ghost. Replace it with Dachigam National Park — open in winter for the hangul deer, ₹1,100 permit, and you'll be the only visitor.

Day 3-4: Gulmarg — the snow, the gondola, the gap

The two-hour drive from Srinagar to Gulmarg: shared cab ₹500/person, private ₹3,500. The road is snow-covered past Tangmarg — chains on tyres, experienced drivers only. Don't self-drive. In Gulmarg, stay at the base (₹4,000-6,000/night for a heated room — not "heated" as in kettle, as in Bukhari stove running all night). The gondola (Phase 1: ₹740, Phase 2: ₹950) runs weather permitting. In January, Phase 2 opens 3 out of 7 days. If it's open, go — you're at 13,950 feet looking down into Pakistan-administered Kashmir. The skiing is the cheapest in the world for the terrain: ₹1,500 full-day ski + boot + pole rental, beginner lesson ₹2,000 for four hours. Gulmarg skiing is not for western beginners — the mountain is avalanche-controlled but not patrol-marked like the Alps. Bring a probe if you have one.

Evenings: kewa (local green tea) in the hotel. Electric power drops out for 2-3 hours daily — the best guesthouses have generators. Ask before you book.

Day 5: Sonmarg — the ice wall and the road closure threat

Sonmarg is 3 hours from Srinagar, open in winter but the town itself closes by November. You go for one thing: the Thajiwas Glacier walk. Walk or pony (₹700-1,200) to the glacier base — 40 minutes. There is no gondola. You get snow, ice caves (seasonal), and often zero other Indians. The road to Sonmarg closes to vehicles from January to March because of avalanche risk. If it's open when you're there, go. If it's closed, replace with Yusmarg — 45 km from Srinagar, meadow under snow, almost unknown to tourists.

Day 6: Srinagar — crafts, kahwa, and Wazwan

Back to Srinagar. The winter is when artisans work and tourists don't interrupt. Visit the paper-mâché workshops in Zadibal (free, you can watch the process). Pashmina authentication at the Government Arts Emporium on Residency Road — do not buy from the shops that "just happen" near your houseboat. Real pashmina in winter 2026: ₹8,000-15,000 for a full-size, machine-test-verifiable shawl (they'll burn a thread for you). The ₹2,500 "100% pashmina" near the lake is not pashmina.

Wazwan: the full Kashmiri feast (rista, gushtaba, tabak maaz, seekh) is served to groups — 7-36 items. It's a celebration meal. In winter, you'll find it at Ahdoos on Residency Road (₹850/person for a plated version). Minimum 2 people. Book ahead.

What it actually costs (December 2026)

6 days in Kashmir, two people, budget-mid: return flights Delhi-Srinagar ₹9,000 per person, stay ₹3,500/night average (mix of houseboat and guesthouse), food ₹800/day/person, gondola + permits + transport ₹5,000 total. Realistic total for two: ₹45,000-55,000. Solo travellers add 30% to the per-night because of double-occupancy guesthouses.

The agent quote you'll get: ₹35,000 "all-inclusive." What that actually means: a shared houseboat with no heating, group transport, and 45 minutes at each viewpoint. The private 6-day winter trip above costs ₹50,000 for two. The ₹35,000 package costs ₹35,000 and you get exactly what you pay for.

Winter-specific packing nobody tells you

1. A thermos. You will want hot kewa on the gondola line. Nobody sells it there. 2. Proper boots — not sneakers. Srinagar in January is slush by day, ice by night. Sports shoes slide. 3. Moisturizer and lip balm — the cold is dry, not wet. Your hands will crack in two days otherwise. 4. A power bank — winter power cuts last 2-4 hours. Your phone is your camera and your torch. 5. Cash. ATMs in Gulmarg run out by evening. Srinagar city ATMs work fine. Bring ₹15,000 in cash from Srinagar.

FAQ

Is it safe to visit Kashmir in winter? Safety incidents have not increased in winter vs summer. The biggest risk is weather — road closures and gondola shutdowns. Check conditions and keep your plan flexible.

Will Dal Lake freeze? Can I walk on it? Dal freezes lightly in late January in patches. Local children walk on the frozen edges. You should not. The ice thickness varies and breaks in spots. Admire from the shikara.

Is Gulmarg worth it if I don't ski? Yes. The gondola, the snow viewpoints, the walks, and the hamlets covered in snow are worth the trip. Skiing is a bonus, not a prerequisite.

What about the political situation? Kashmir is administered as a Union Territory. Tourist areas are open. Restrictions, if any, are announced and are time-limited. The tourist police number is 7051909393 — note it before you travel.
