On-Device AI on Phones: What It Actually Does in 2026 (and What Is Still a Demo)

By Swami Chavan·Published 2026-11-04·7 min read

The phrase 'AI phone' now appears on every launch slide, and buyers reasonably ask what changes in daily use. The honest answer: a few things genuinely improved, several things are cloud features wearing a local label, and one or two are still party tricks.

What on-device AI does well today

  • Photo processing. Noise reduction, HDR merging and portrait edge detection run locally and are fast. This is the most mature AI on any phone.
  • Live transcription and call summaries. Works offline on current flagships and mid-tier chips with a capable NPU.
  • Text actions. Rewriting, summarising and correcting text inside the keyboard or notes app, without sending it anywhere.
  • Object removal in photos. Reliable for small distractions; visibly imperfect for large removals.

What still needs the cloud

Long-form summarisation, complex multi-step tasks, high-quality image generation and anything requiring a large model still run on servers. That matters for two reasons: it costs data, and it exposes your input to a provider. When a feature is described as 'AI-powered' without saying where it runs, assume cloud.

The NPU number game

Marketing now quotes TOPS — trillions of operations per second — as if it predicted usefulness. It does not. What matters is whether the specific features you want run locally and how much battery they consume. A phone with a modest NPU and well-optimised software beats a big number with poor integration.

Should AI influence your buying decision?

Only if you will use transcription, text rewriting or heavy photo editing. For calls, UPI, social media and camera shots in good light, AI features are not the deciding factor — battery, display and update commitment still are.

The verdict

Buy a phone for battery, display and updates. Treat AI as a bonus that is genuinely useful for transcription and photos, and remember that half of what is advertised runs somewhere else.

Quick answers

Do AI phone features work without internet?

Some do — photo processing, live transcription and text rewriting on newer chipsets run locally. Summarisation and image generation usually need a connection.

Does on-device AI drain the battery?

Local AI tasks spike power briefly during processing but avoid the radio cost of uploading data. Sustained local AI use, such as long transcription sessions, does warm the phone.

Is an NPU worth paying extra for in 2026?

Only if you use transcription, text tools or heavy photo editing. Otherwise put the money into battery, display quality and update years.
