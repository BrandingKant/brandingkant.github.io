Thailand visa on arrival for Indians is easy — but the counter has rules nobody posts about.

By Swami Chavan · Published 4 October 2026 · 7 min read

The Thailand visa on arrival counter at Bangkok Suvarnabhumi is the first tourist experience most Indians have in the country, and it sets the tone for everything after. The good news: it works, it's fast, and 95% of people walk through in under 40 minutes. The bad news: the 5% who get pulled aside almost always made the same three mistakes. I've crossed that counter four times. Here's exactly what happens at the window.

Before you fly: the documents that actually matter

Passport valid 6+ months with 2 blank pages. Return ticket within 15 days. One passport photo (4×6 cm). 2,200 THB fee (₹5,400) in cash — the counter prefers Thai baht; carry exact change. Proof of hotel for at least the first night. And the form — fill it on the plane, not at the counter where 200 people are queueing behind you.

What happens at the counter, step by step

Step 1: The immigration officer checks your form and photo. Step 2: You pay 2,200 THB at the fee window — some counters take cards but the machine "being down" is a regular character in this story. Step 3: You wait in the seating area while they process — this is where the return ticket and hotel proof get checked. Step 4: Stamped, 15 days. Total: 25–40 minutes on a normal day.

The three things that get Indians pulled aside

1. No return ticket. This is not a formality — they check. If you're doing an open-ended Southeast Asia run, book a refundable onward flight out of Thailand. 2. Solo travellers with no hotel booking, carrying very little cash. It's unfair, it's profiling, and it still happens — carry a hotel printout. 3. The photo. Airport kiosks charge 200 THB for a "visa photo." Bring your own and keep 200 baht for street pad thai instead.

The 15-day trap

Visa on arrival gives 15 days — extendable once by 7 days at an immigration office for 1,900 THB, no questions asked. That's 22 days maximum. If you're planning a month in Thailand, the 60-day tourist visa from the Thai Embassy in New Delhi, Chennai, or Kolkata costs ₹6,000-ish and saves you the entire counter dance. Do that instead.

After the stamp

Take the Airport Rail Link into the city (45 THB) instead of a taxi (400–500 THB plus tolls). A tourist SIM at the airport is 299–599 THB for 8–15 days — AIS and DTAC counters are right after arrivals. And change money inside the city at SuperRich exchange rates, never the airport booths.

FAQ

Is visa on arrival guaranteed? No visa is. Thailand rejects very few Indians, but insufficient documents (especially no return ticket) are the reason when it happens. Carry everything and you're fine.

Can I apply online first? Yes — Thailand's e-VOA lets you pre-apply and use a fast lane for a small extra fee. Worth it in December–January peak season.

How much cash should I carry? 10,000 THB per person or 20,000 per family is the official requirement they can ask to see. In practice they rarely ask, but a couple showing a working ATM card is the realistic version of proof.

Is Phuket different from Bangkok? Same process, shorter queues, same documents.

The Traveltico dossier: this place, properly

Location history & important places

Bangkok was founded in 1782 when King Rama I moved the capital across the Chao Phraya after the sacking of Ayutthaya, the old capital 80 km north — its ruined temples are a day trip from the city. Important places: the Grand Palace and Wat Phra Kaew (home of the Emerald Buddha), Wat Pho with its 46-metre reclining Buddha, and Wat Arun across the river.

The local myth

The Jade Buddha at Wat Phra Kaew is said to have been created in India in 43 BC by a sage with the help of the god Vishnu — a story no historian can verify, but it explains why this small statue has its own changing-of-seasons ceremony presided over by the King himself.

Special points

The wai — palms together — is a greeting and a thank-you at once. Never point your feet at a Buddha image or touch anyone's head. And respect for the monarchy is law, not etiquette: skip the jokes entirely.

Highlights

Chatuchak weekend market (15,000 stalls, go early), a khlong boat through the old canals for the pre-skyscraper city, and Wat Arun at sunset from a riverside bar for under 100 baht.

Why this trip creates connections

Thais live by sanuk — the belief that anything worth doing should be fun, together. Street food is eaten at shared tables, massages are communal, and a stranger who returns a wai with a smile has already made five friends.

Quick answers

What is the Thailand visa on arrival fee for Indians?

The visa on arrival costs 2,200 THB in cash at the counter, grants 15 days, and cannot be extended beyond that.

What documents do Indians need at the Thailand VOA counter?

Passport valid 6+ months, one passport photo, the filled TM87 form, a confirmed return ticket within 15 days, hotel booking and proof of about 10,000 THB per person.

Is visa on arrival or e-visa better for Indians?

The 60-day e-visa applied online is better for trips longer than 15 days; the VOA only suits short visits and the counter queue can take over an hour.

The famous food of Bangkok

The dishes this destination is actually known for — what to order, where locals eat it, and what it costs.

Tom yum goong

Hot-sour shrimp soup with lemongrass — 60-120 baht at street stalls; the dish that teaches Thai flavour in one spoon.

Pad thai

The stir-fry standard at Thipsamai since 1966 — 60-80 baht; the queue is the quality signal.

Mango sticky rice

Coconut cream, ripe mango, warm rice — 80-120 baht; March-June mango season is when it earns its fame.
