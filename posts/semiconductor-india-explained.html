India's Semiconductor Push, Explained Without the Jargon

By Swami Chavan·Published 2026-11-04·7 min read

Every few weeks there is another semiconductor headline from India, and most readers reasonably ask: so do we make chips now or not? The honest answer is 'some, and far fewer than the headlines imply'. Here is the state of play without the press-release language.

The three layers everyone confuses

  • Design — writing the chip's blueprint. India has done this for decades; nearly every major chip company has a large engineering centre here.
  • Fabrication — physically printing circuits onto silicon wafers. This is the capital-intensive layer, measured in billions of dollars per plant.
  • Assembly, testing and packaging (ATMP) — cutting, bonding and testing finished chips. Lower capital, faster to build, and where India's first projects have concentrated.

Most announcements that sound like fabs are ATMP plants or design centres. That is not a downgrade — packaging is a real and growing part of the value chain — but it is not the same as printing advanced logic chips.

Why fabrication is genuinely hard to start

A modern fab needs ultra-pure water in enormous volumes, uninterrupted power, vibration-free foundations, and a supply chain of chemicals and gases that must arrive reliably. A single particle of dust can ruin a wafer. This is why countries spend a decade building the ecosystem around a plant before the plant is competitive.

What it means for what you pay

In the near term, very little. Phone and laptop prices follow global memory and logic pricing, not local plant openings. The realistic benefit of a domestic ecosystem is downstream: component suppliers, design jobs, and a shorter supply chain during global shortages. Those effects take years to reach a price tag.

The signals worth watching

  • Announcements that name a technology node and a customer — vague 'advanced chips' language usually means packaging.
  • Supplier ecosystems: chemical, gas and equipment companies setting up nearby.
  • Talent movement: process engineers being hired in hundreds, not tens.
  • Power and water agreements actually signed, not proposed.

The honest summary

India is building the foundation of a semiconductor industry, layer by layer, and the foundation is real. The building is not finished, and any claim that it is should be read the way you read a product launch event: directionally true, specifically exaggerated.

The verdict

Design and packaging are moving; advanced fabrication is a multi-year project. Watch suppliers, node names and hiring numbers instead of headline value figures. That is where the real progress shows.

Quick answers

Does India manufacture chips today?

India has assembly, testing and packaging facilities and a very large chip design workforce. Advanced logic fabrication at cutting-edge nodes is still being built, not operating.

Will local chip manufacturing make phones cheaper?

Not directly or quickly. Device prices follow global component pricing. Domestic manufacturing mainly improves supply resilience and creates engineering jobs over the long term.

What is ATMP?

Assembly, Testing, Marking and Packaging — the stage where a finished silicon wafer is cut into individual chips, connected to a package and tested. It is less capital-intensive than fabrication and is where India's earliest projects are concentrated.
