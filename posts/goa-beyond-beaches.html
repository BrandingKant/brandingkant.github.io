Goa beyond the shacks: the Goa that locals actually keep for themselves

By Swami Chavan · Published 4 October 2026 · 7 min read

Every December, India floods into the same 4 kilometres of Baga–Calangute and calls it Goa. Meanwhile the state is 105 kilometres of coastline with three different cultures, and the best parts have no DJs. I've spent a combined four months there across monsoons and seasons. Here's the version of Goa worth the trip.

Pick a side first

North Goa: crowded, cheap, loud, fun once a year. South Goa: quiet, cleaner beaches, better hotels, the actual point. My standing recommendation for anyone over 25: stay south (Agonda, Palolem, Cola), party north once, go home rested instead of needing a vacation from the vacation.

The beaches worth the journey

Cola Beach — the blue lagoon beach with the freshwater creek meeting the sea; tents on the cliff, zero buildings. Ashwem — what Anjuna was in 2008. Butterfly Beach — reachable by boat or a 30-minute forest hike, and the best sunset in the state. Keri (Querim) — the northernmost strip, where Goa ends and the emptiness begins. None of these have a shack playing 'Gupt' remixes.

The stuff that isn't beaches

Old Goa's Bom Jesus Basilica — UNESCO, 400+ years, and the actual remains of St. Francis Xavier on public display. Fontainhas in Panjim — Latin Quarter, Portuguese houses in ochre and indigo, and the best photo walk in India that isn't a monument. Divar island by ferry from Old Goa — the Goa of 1970, one village, zero traffic. Chandor and its mando nights if you can find an invite. Spices plantations in Ponda — touristy but a solid half-day, with an elephant-free elephant-free lunch of xacudi that justifies the ticket.

The food rules

1. No menus with photos. 2. Beach shacks are for drinks, not meals — the plate is a tax. 3. The real meals: fish thali at a local lunch home (₹150–250), poi from the village baker at 6 AM (₹15, warm, gone by 7), chorizo pao at a Petisco-style kitchen, and urrak in season (March–May, the cashew feni's younger sibling that everyone drinks and nobody ships). 4. If the restaurant has a queue of locals at 1 PM, that's dinner solved.

Practical math (October 2026)

Stay: South Goa guesthouses ₹2,000–4,000/night even in December if booked by October; north triples by Christmas. Scooter rental: ₹400–600/day — the entire state is a scooter away. Fuel, food, stay for a 4-day trip: ₹10,000–15,000 per person. Goa is the cheapest good holiday in India if you don't do New Year week.

FAQ

Best time for Goa? November–February for the classic season; June–September monsoon for the empty, green, 30%-price version. Never Dec 25–Jan 5 if peace is the goal.

North or South for first-timers? South. Do a one-day north trip for the market (Anjuna, Wednesdays) and Arpora night market.

Is Goa safe for solo women? Tourist Goa is fine with standard city caution; the interior villages are safer than most Indian cities. The police tourist helpline (1323) actually works.

Do I need a car? A scooter. Goa without independent wheels is a series of taxi negotiations.
