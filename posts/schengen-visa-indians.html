Schengen visa rejection for Indians is at an all-time high. The applicants who get approved do these five things.

By Swami Chavan · Published 5 October 2026 · 10 min read

Schengen visa rejection rates for Indians rose sharply in 2024-2026. France alone rejected over 20% of Indian applications in some months. The rejection isn't random — it lands on applications that miss specific documents, weak financial proof, or unclear purpose. If you're applying for the first time, the process is not complicated but it is unforgiving. One missing paper and you lose ₹7,000 and three weeks. Here's the full first-timer process, what actually causes rejection, and how to be in the approved pile.

What a Schengen visa actually is

A Schengen visa (Type C, short-stay) lets you travel across 29 European countries for up to 90 days in any 180-day period. The countries include France, Germany, Italy, Spain, Netherlands, Greece, Switzerland, Austria, Belgium, Portugal, and 19 more. You apply through the embassy of the country that is your main destination — the most nights. If your nights are equal, apply through the country of first entry. You cannot shop embassies for the easiest one. If your itinerary is 4 nights Paris, 3 nights Rome, you apply through France. The embassy decides.

Step 1: Choose the right embassy

The most common mistake: picking the embassy that processes fastest, not the embassy of your actual main destination. Embassies share data. VFS Global shares data. If you apply to Italy with an itinerary that's clearly 5 nights in France, you get rejected for "wrong jurisdiction" — and you're out the fee.

Popular embassy routes for Indian first-timers: 1. France (via VFS, Delhi/Mumbai/Bangalore/Kolkata/Chennai) — high rejection rate but high volume. 2. Italy (via VFS, six cities) — moderate rejection, slower processing (15-20 days). 3. Switzerland (via VFS, the Switzerland embassy in Delhi has its own system) — lowest rejection rate for first-timers, but requires you to actually have Switzerland as the main stay. 4. Netherlands (via VFS) — moderate, efficient.

Contrarian call: for first-timers, Switzerland has the cleanest approval record. If your itinerary genuinely centers on Switzerland (Lucerne, Interlaken, Zermatt), apply there. France and Italy get the volume because everyone passes through them — but they also get the most rejection.

Step 2: The documents that actually decide

The Schengen checklist is identical across embassies — the differences are in how strictly each embassy verifies. Here's what actually moves approvals:

1. Bank statements (last 6 months). Not 3. Six months. The consulate wants to see sustained financial stability, not a sudden ₹2,00,000 deposit two weeks before the application. If your balance was ₹15,000 five months ago and ₹3,00,000 now, the embassy asks for the source and often rejects on this basis alone. Make sure the average balance across 6 months is at least ₹1,50,000. No large unexplained deposits.

2. ITR (Income Tax Return) — last 3 years. Not 1. Three years. Filed regularly, no missed years. If you're a freelancer or recently employed, file ITR even if your income is below the taxable threshold — the filing, not the amount, matters.

3. Salary slips (last 3 months, company letterhead, stamped). If self-employed: company registration, GST registration, and an undertaking letter on letterhead. The undertaking should state that the business will not suffer from your absence, and that you're expected to return.

4. Leave approval letter (HR department, company seal). This is the single most under-rated document. It confirms employment and returnability. Without it, rejection probability rises sharply.

5. Travel insurance (€30,000 minimum coverage, Schengen area). ₹1,500-2,500 for 15 days. Buy any Schengen-approved policy. Tata AIG, HDFC Ergo, ICICI Lombard all work.

6. Flight reservation (not paid ticket). Use a hold option via Yatra or your agent — they issue a reservation that auto-cancels in 72 hours. Don't pay for the flight before visa approval.

7. Hotel reservations (all nights, every city). Not one hotel for the entire trip — each booking should cover the nights in that city. Booking.com's free cancellation works. After the visa, change what you want.

8. Cover letter (one page). State purpose of visit, destinations, dates, financial stability, ties to India (job, family, business). Hand-write nothing — typed, signed, with passport number and date.

9. Passport-size photos (35x45mm, white background, 80% face). Don't reuse an old photo. Schengen photos are biometric-checked.

10. Passport (valid 3 months beyond return, two blank pages, no older visas on a passport older than 10 years — Schengen does not accept passports issued 10+ years ago).

Step 3: The appointment and fee

VFS Global handles Schengen applications for most embassies. Book the appointment on the VFS website (vfsglobal.com) — slots open 2-4 weeks in advance. The visa fee: €90 (₹8,100) for adults, €45 for children 6-12. VFS charges an additional ₹1,500-2,000 service fee. Pay by card/online. Cash not accepted at most centers.

Processing time: typically 15 days. Switzerland can be 10. France currently varies from 12 to 25 days. Do not buy non-refundable tickets before approval.

What causes rejection (the reasons the embassy won't say)

1. Insufficient funds. Average 6-month balance below ₹1L. Even if the current balance is ₹3L, the history matters.

2. Unclear purpose. Cover letter doesn't explain the trip. A one-line "tourism" cover letter is a rejection flag. Explain your itinerary.

3. Weak ties to India. Single, employed but vague about return. The leave approval letter is your anchor.

4. Fake documents. Bank statements edited to show higher balance. The embassy calls banks. Faking financials gets you banned from Schengen for years.

5. Historical visa non-use. If you have an old Schengen visa and didn't use it (didn't travel), the next application is harder. Only apply when you're genuinely going.

6. Mismatched itinerary. The destinations on your cover letter don't match your hotel bookings. VFS officers check this before submission.

Step 4: The interview (some embassies, not all)

France and Germany occasionally call for interviews. Switzerland and Italy rarely do for first-timers. If called, the questions are about your itinerary: where are you going, why, how will you fund it, when will you return. Stick to your cover letter. Don't add destinations. Don't speculate. If you don't know an answer, say you don't know. Lying is rejection.

Step 5: After approval

Your visa will specify the validity dates and the number of entries (single, double, multiple). Do not overstay — the next application tracks that. Always carry your travel insurance, flight, and hotel reservations on your phone when entering Schengen. Border agents can ask for them.

What it actually costs (2026)

Schengen visa: €90 (₹8,100). VFS service fee: ₹1,500-2,500. Photos: ₹200 (Kodak Express or local studio — ask for Schengen format). Travel insurance: ₹1,800. Courier return of passport: ₹500. Total fixed: ₹12,000-13,000. The trip itself is separate.

FAQ

Should I apply through the country with the lowest rejection rate? No. Apply through the embassy of your actual main destination. Wrong jurisdiction is a rejection reason.

Can I apply without an ITR? Technically yes, but rejection probability is high. File an ITR, even as a freelancer — the filing record matters more than the income amount.

How much bank balance is enough? There's no official minimum. Aim for an average of ₹1,50,000 over 6 months with consistent income. A sudden spike hurts.

If I have a valid US or UK visa, does that help? It's a positive signal but not a guarantee. Some embassies fast-track return travelers, but the same documents apply.

How early should I apply? No earlier than 6 months before your trip, no later than 15 days. Embassies recommend applying 3-4 weeks before your travel date.
